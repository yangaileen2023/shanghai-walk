Route 01
The Stylish West

Tree-lined lanes, art deco villas, the best independent coffee in the city, and the street that gave Shanghai its reputation for effortless cool.

5 hours~6 kmMostly flatFrench Concession
Stops along the way
01
Wukang Road & Wukang Mansion
Start at the iconic fan-shaped building from 1924. The whole street is lined with French plane trees and restored villas — ideal for a slow morning stroll.
02
Anfu Road
Shanghai's most beloved café strip. Pick up a coffee and watch the city's creative crowd cycle past. Baker & Spice is a local institution here.
03
Wulumuqi Road
A quieter lane with independent bookshops, vintage stores, and neighbourhood restaurants that haven't changed in decades.
04
Fuxing Park
The French Concession's open-air living room. On weekday mornings you'll find tai chi, card games, and old men playing erhu — genuinely Shanghai.
Where we eat & drink
Baker & Spice (Anfu Rd)
Sourdough, pastries, excellent iced coffee. Sit outside if it's not too hot.
Egg (Xinle Rd)
Tiny brunch spot beloved by expats and locals alike. Order the eggs benedict.
Liquid Laundry
Craft beer and wood-fired pizza near Fuxing Park — good for a late afternoon wind-down.
Street tanghulu
Candied hawthorn on a stick from the park entrance vendor. Non-negotiable.
Best seasons
Winter
Quiet, bare trees
Spring
Trees bloom — stunning
Autumn
Golden leaves
Summer
35°C+, humid

Peak: March–May and Oct–Nov. The plane trees make this route magical in autumn.

The Bund · Shanghai
Route 02
Old Meets New

The world-famous waterfront, six centuries of Chinese garden design, and the street food lanes where Shanghai's soul still lives.

5 hours~7 kmEasy paceHuangpu District
Stops along the way
01
The Bund (Waitan)
Start at the promenade when morning light hits the Pudong skyline. Over 50 heritage buildings line this 1.5 km stretch — each one worth stopping for.
02
Nanjing Road Pedestrian Street
The pulse of the city. Department stores, street vendors, local snack chains. Head inland to escape the tourist flow and find where locals actually shop.
03
Yu Garden (Yuyuan)
A Ming dynasty garden built in 1559. Rockeries, koi ponds, zigzag bridges. Arrive before 10am to avoid the crowds. The teahouse inside is worth a sit-down.
04
Old City lanes
The Chenxiangge area around Yu Garden — narrow lanes with street food stalls, wet markets, and neighbourhood life that's vanishing fast.
Where we eat & drink
Nanxiang Steamed Bun Restaurant
Legendary xiaolongbao beside Yu Garden. Queue is worth it — order the crab roe ones.
Jia Jia Tang Bao
Humbler, cheaper, arguably better. A true local institution for soup dumplings.
Old Jesse (Tianping Rd)
Classic Shanghainese home cooking — braised pork belly, drunken chicken, stir-fried river shrimp.
Scallion pancakes (cong you bing)
From any street cart near the old city. Hot, flaky, ¥5. Shanghai's best snack.
Best seasons
Winter
Low crowds
Spring
Mild & bright
Autumn
Ideal walking
Summer
Packed & hot

Yu Garden is closed on Mondays. The Bund before 9am has the best light and fewest people.

Tianzifang · Shanghai
Route 03
Cool & Underground

Shikumen alleyways reimagined, art studios, designers you won't find in any travel guide, and the rooftop views that justify the walk.

5 hours~5.5 kmLeisurelyLuwan / Xuhui
Stops along the way
01
Xintiandi
Old shikumen stone-gate houses reimagined as restaurants, galleries and boutiques. The architecture is genuinely impressive — old bricks, new money, interesting tension.
02
Tianzifang
A warren of tiny lanes with ceramics studios, indie designers, and rooftop workshops. Go deeper than the main lane — the good stuff is always one more turn away.
03
Huaihai Road
Shanghai's answer to Champs-Élysées. International flagships next to local fashion labels and concept stores. The sycamore trees overhead make it feel like a film set.
04
K11 Musea
A shopping mall where every floor has serious contemporary art installations. The rooftop terrace gives you the Huangpu and Pudong at once — unbeatable for a late-afternoon view.
Where we eat & drink
Chua Lam's Dim Sum (Xintiandi)
Elevated HK-style dim sum in a beautiful setting. The har gow is exceptional.
Wagas
Fresh bowls, salads, and serious smoothies. Good for a midwalk refuel.
Taiko (Tianzifang)
Japanese-inspired small plates in a laneway setting. Order the black sesame soft serve after.
K11 Rooftop Bar
End the walk here with a cocktail and the city spread out below. Golden hour is the moment.
Best seasons
Winter
Quiet & cosy
Spring
Perfect temp
Autumn
Best for photos
Summer
Very crowded

Best on weekday mornings before Tianzifang fills up. Weekend afternoons in summer can feel overwhelming in the narrow lanes.
